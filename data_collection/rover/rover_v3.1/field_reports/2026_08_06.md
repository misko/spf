Covers 2026-08-06, bench and pre-field. All times are the fleet's local time
(Europe/London) unless stated. No missions were flown; this is a preparation and
diagnosis day.

**No capture data. Thirteen commits, three retracted conclusions, and one defect
that has been silently mis-dating 40% of the campaign since day one.**

Three things came out of today that change how earlier data should be read:

## Part 2 — The receive-path investigation, and what it actually showed

### 2.1 WITHDRAWN: "channel 0 is deaf on every radio, fleet-wide"

Earlier today I reported that ch0 had never received the emitter on any rover on
any day, supported by an emitter-band SNR of 0–2 dB across 31 receiver-captures,
and a coherence between elements at the emitter IF of ~0.005.

**That is wrong, and the method was wrong.** The O4 is a bursty digital video
transmitter, not a CW source — `data_quality_plan.md` records 60–70% NaN as the
healthy baseline for it. Every statistic I computed took a **median over all
frames**, so with the emitter silent in most frames the median measured the gaps
between bursts, not the signal. The "noise reference" band I chose also sat
inside the emitter's own 3 MHz bandwidth.

Per frame, the picture inverts:

| receiver | ch0 burst swing | ch0 loud−quiet | ch1 swing | corr(ch0,ch1) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| RO1 r0 | 19.8 dB | +12.8 | 13.9 | +0.71 |
| RO1 r1 | 20.5 dB | +11.5 | 10.6 | +0.61 |
| RO3 r0 | 19.6 dB | +11.4 | 14.2 | +0.64 |
| RO3 r1 | 20.4 dB | +12.0 | 10.9 | +0.68 |
| RO4 r0 | 18.3 dB | +10.7 | 12.2 | +0.36 |
| **RO4 r1** | **3.3 dB** | **+2.0** | 9.8 | +0.54 |

ch0 swings ~20 dB between burst and gap and is the *more* responsive element on
every healthy receiver; both channels rise and fall together. The one genuinely
flat element was **RO4 r1 ch0** — 3.3 dB swing, the Aug 4 missing-pole antenna.

### 2.2 WITHDRAWN: "ch0 died when the fleet moved to 5.766 GHz"

Follow-on claim, same method, same error. Checked against the 5.866 GHz and
2.467 GHz eras it appeared to hold, but per-frame those eras show ch0 bursting
at 16.7–18.9 dB — identical to 5.766. The earlier campaign also used a
**CW "tone blaster"** emitter, present in *every* frame, which is why medians
showed signal there and not on the O4 days. **There is no frequency effect.**

### 2.3 What does stand

- `rover radio signal` compares channels **across all frames**, so with a bursty
  emitter it measures the silence. This is the root of every wrong turn above
  and it is still unfixed — see S-6.
- The metric it calls `railed_fraction` is `(gain == max(gain)).mean()`, i.e.
  the fraction of frames at the *maximum observed* gain, **not** at the hardware
  ceiling. For a constant gain it reports 100% regardless. It is a good
  frozen-AGC detector wearing the wrong name (Part 6.2).

## Part 3 — Capture filenames have been wrong since day one of the campaign

### 3.1 The measurement

Comparing every finalised campaign capture's filename against the minimum
`gps_timestamp` inside its own store:

| day | misdated >3 min | worst |
|---|---:|---|
| 07-31 | 3 | +2h03m |
| 08-01 | 3 | +57m |
| 08-02 | 4 | +51m |
| 08-04 | 5 | **+8h38m** |
| 08-05 | 4 | +4h27m |
| **total** | **19 of 47** | |

Rover 4's 08-04 entries are mostly the separate PDT-vs-BST timezone bug (O3,
since fixed by the reconciler); the rest are the defect below.

### 3.2 The cause

The Pi 4 has no battery-backed RTC, so a network-less boot restores the
timestamp saved at last shutdown (journald: `System clock time unset or jumped
backwards, restoring from recorded timestamp`). The capture filename is stamped
from that clock at process start (`mavlink_radio_collection.py:254`) — minutes
before the first sample is recorded.

`drone_run.sh` then declined to ask the GPS:

```bash
system_clock_is_plausible() {
    # Raspberry Pi 5 has an RTC and may also have NTP. A plausible clock is
    # sufficient for boot filenames; ...
    [[ "$(date +%s)" -ge 1735689600 ]]  # 2025-01-01T00:00:00Z
}
```

Both premises are false for this fleet: these are **Pi 4s** (no RTC), and
"plausible" meant "later than 1 Jan 2025", which a clock restored to four hours
ago clears by nineteen months. Introduced by `920bc63` *"Speed rover boot to
navigation wait"* (2026-07-31 05:54) as a boot-latency optimisation — the first
day of the v7 campaign.

The rover was carrying correct UTC on the MAVLink link the whole time
(`SYSTEM_TIME.time_unix_usec`, from ArduPilot's GPS) and writing it into every
snapshot, while naming the file from a clock read off disk.

### 3.3 The blast radius, precisely

**Merged datasets are sound.** `v7_tx_rx_merge.py` interpolates TX position onto
RX `gps_timestamp`, never onto filenames. Bearings and ground truth are correct.

**What is broken** is filenames as an ordering or identification key, day
attribution (Part 11 of the Aug 5 report already counts by GPS date for this
reason), and correlating a capture to journal entries. The merge is also a full
TX×RX cross-product over whatever it is handed — it does no session curation —
so *selecting* captures by filename can silently mis-group them.

### 3.4 Fixed

Every boot now obtains GPS time before anything is named, and again before each
capture. Deliberately unbounded in attempts is *not* the design: three bounded
tries, then a loud warning and a boot that continues, because a rover with no
GPS cannot start a mission anyway.

A latent epoch-0 bug had to be closed at the same time, because making the sync
mandatory promoted it from unreachable to per-boot-reachable: `--get-time` used
to exit on a 3D fix even with no UTC, writing epoch 0, and the shell's `1970-*`
guard is timezone-dependent (`1970-01-01 01:00:00` in Europe/London but
`1969-12-31 16:00:00` west of UTC, where it misses entirely). Both layers fixed;
validation is now on a parsed epoch.

## Part 4 — Rover 4's external compass

Symptom: MAVLink comes up, then `PreArm: Compass not healthy`; on one boot the
external IST8310 (device ID 658953) was absent entirely and the policy gate
failed, killing the collector.

### 4.1 It is physical, and it was introduced on 2026-08-06

| | Aug 5 (6 boots) | Aug 6 (last 4 boots) |
|---|---|---|
| Compass policy PASS | **6 / 6** | gate ran once → **FAILED** |
| `PreArm: Compass not healthy` | **0** | **3 of 4 boots** |
| External 658953 absent | 0 | 1 boot |

Same firmware, same parameters, same calibration (offset norm 1009.7 mG on all
seven recorded occurrences — no recalibration). The only change in between was
physical work on the vehicle. A boot-timing race would have failed yesterday too.

### 4.2 Rover 4 has exactly one compass

`COMPASS_PRIO2_ID=658945` (I2C bus 0) and `PRIO3_ID=131594` (SPI) are configured
but have **never** been detected; `COMPASS_USE2/USE3` are both 0, so they are
inert warnings. Decoded, 658953 = `0xA0E09` = IST8310 on I2C bus 1, addr 0x0E,
external. When it drops there is no fallback — hence "not healthy" rather than a
silent demotion to an internal compass. The phantom IDs match rover 3's own
parameter dumps, suggesting rover 4's FC inherited them at bring-up.

### 4.3 The gate now retries, and only an absence is retried

`compass_policy` classifies its own errors. `retryable` is true only when
**every** error is "the external compass is not on the bus". A wrong
`COMPASS_PRIO1_ID`, a nonzero `COMPASS_DISBLMSK` or an out-of-limit offset reads
identically after any number of reboots, so retrying it would bury a real defect
behind a delay. An absence *plus* a misconfiguration is not retryable.

Ladder: one check, then 3 retries — settle 5–10 s and re-read parameters (no
reboot), then FC reboot + 15–25 s, then FC reboot + 30–45 s. Delays are
randomised so retries cannot land in lockstep with a periodic condition, and so
four rovers restarting together do not retry in phase. The first attempt does
not reboot because the gate reads `COMPASS_DEV_ID*`, which the FC populates
during its boot probe — a check run shortly after an FC restart can report a
healthy compass as absent, and ruling that out costs seconds rather than ~40 s.

Then it escalates to a **full rover reboot**, unbounded by request, with a 20 s
announced window so an operator can intervene. `SPF_COMPASS_REBOOT_ENABLE=0`
parks a rover with a known-bad lead instead of cycling.

| RO4 r1 | −43.4 | −47.9 | 29.4 / 34.5 | 4.5 dB → OK |

### 6.1 The gain column is the headline

RO1 r0 ch0 in its Aug 5 *mission* capture read **−83.2 dB at 61.4 dB gain,
railed 90%**. It now reads **−50.9 at 36.8 dB, railed 1%**, and its imbalance is
7.0 dB rather than 26.7 dB.

Caution against over-reading that: on Aug 5 the weak channel sat at the noise
floor with the AGC railed, and you cannot measure how far down something is once
it is below the floor. A strong local emitter lifts it off, and the *true*
imbalance shows. Some of 27 dB → 7 dB is the signal being stronger, not the path
being better. The residual 6–9 dB matches the fleet baseline already documented
in `rx_signal_metrics.py` ("6-11 dB as of 2026-08-04").

Note also that the weak channel **flips side** between rovers — ch0 on RO1, ch1
on RO3 and RO4 — which rules out a systematic per-channel-index deficit.

### 6.2 Rover 3: a frozen AGC, and a wrong diagnosis

RO3 r1 ch1 (**B1**, stern) initially reported `railed 100%` at a median gain of
29 dB. The raw gains across 100 frames:

```
r0 ch0 (A0):  {28:86, 29:13, 30:1}                           AGC working
r0 ch1 (A1):  {35:88, 36:1, 37:10, 40:1}                     AGC working
r1 ch0 (B0):  {26:20, 27:35, 28:23, 29:14, 30:3, 33:3, 34:1, 62:1}
r1 ch1 (B1):  {29:100}                                       FROZEN
```

The gain was **constant at 29 dB for every frame** — not at the 62 dB rail.

I attributed this to the fast_attack gain-lock the config comment warns about
(*"persistent fast-attack gain-lock states seen on bursty digital video"*).
**That was wrong.** An antenna was replaced, `fast_attack` was left unchanged,
and the channel now varies normally (railed 5%), r1 imbalance 4.3 → 0.7 dB and
r0 9.0 → 3.7 dB. The rover PASSES.

The mechanism makes sense in hindsight: an antenna not coupling to the field
gives the AGC nothing varying to track, so the loop settles and stays. The A/B
is therefore uncontaminated and rover 3 remains the fast_attack arm.

## Part 11 — After the session: what the logs showed, and what was fixed

Pulled the full journals from all four rovers. Two defects sat on the boundary
between "the planner is driving" and "the collector is recording", and both
mean recorded data describes something other than what it claims.

**The planner was not in control when it said it was.** `planner_in_control`
was assigned at the BOTTOM of the waypoint loop, so it stayed false through the
drive-to-home and the entire first waypoint. Rover 1 logged `Planner starting to
issue move commands` at 01:12:42 and was still logging `Waiting for drone to
start moving` at 01:14:27 — **two minutes of driving with nothing recorded.**
Fixed in `67f1738`.

**The collector recorded straight through operator takeovers.** The flag was
read exactly once, to decide when to START, and never again. Rover 4 advanced
320 → 578 of 3000 records with the mode column reading MANUAL: a stationary
rover, writing snapshots whose `gps_lat`/`gps_long` barely move — and those are
the fields `rx_pos`/`tx_pos` ground truth is derived from.

The first attempt at this (`ee4d1c5`) added the accounting and shipped green,
but was **inert**: `planner_in_control` is a latch that nothing clears when the
vehicle leaves GUIDED, so the signal the new watchdog watched never moved. The
tests fed it synthetic booleans and asserted only the arithmetic. Mode is now
read live from the heartbeat, which also makes resume free — hand control back
and control returns with no state to unwind.

**Navigation health is now counted separately.** "Nobody was driving" and "we
were driving but did not know where we were" are different faults with different
fixes. Captures carry `planner_control_lost_seconds` and
`navigation_unhealthy_seconds`; a GPS or compass failure makes records *wrong*,
not merely uninformative.

### SITL interruption matrix

`tests/test_in_simulator.py` now interrupts a real ArduPilot mid-GUIDED and
requires the collector to notice **and resume**. Measured against the sim:

| Interruption | Injection | Noticed | Resumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operator takes MANUAL | `--mode manual` | ~1 s, `PLANNER CONTROL LOST` | ~3 s on handback |
| GPS loss | `SIM_GPS_DISABLE=1` | ~2 s, EKF unhealthy | navigation recovers; **control does not** |
| Compass loss | `SIM_MAG1_FAIL=1` | <1 s, `3D_MAG` unhealthy | ~1 s |
| Repeated handbacks | manual/guided ×2 | both noticed | both resumed |

A capture that never trusts itself again after one blip is as unusable as one
that never noticed, so recovery is asserted, not just detection.

**GPS loss is not self-healing.** ArduPilot drops the rover into HOLD on the EKF
failsafe and restoring GPS leaves it there; the planner only recovers via the
stall watchdog's handover, which waits for an operator. Correct behaviour — an
EKF failsafe should not silently resume itself — but a GPS dropout in the field
ends that capture's useful portion until someone intervenes.

### S-1 and S-2 are closed

`rover radio signal` had four defects, and it produced three of the conclusions
retracted in Part 2. Input-referral was per *capture* rather than per *frame*,
which against a bursty emitter and a railing AGC read −81.1 dB where the truth
was −50.0 — the same magnitude as the −83.2 dB that started the "ch0 is deaf
fleet-wide" claim. It errs both ways: the same run reported a 1.5 dB imbalance
where the truth was 6.0. `railed_fraction` was the fraction at the maximum
*observed* gain, so rover 3's frozen 29 dB read as "railed 100%". Every ch1
carries an LO-leakage DC spur that was being counted as antenna signal.

The fourth is the one that matters operationally: **level alone cannot tell a
weak antenna from a strong local source.** On the RO1 capture behind the
withdrawn claim, ch0 sits 20.9 dB *below* ch1 and the tool said DEAD — while ch0
swings 18.7 dB with the emitter's bursts and ch1 swings 2.4 dB. The higher-level
channel is the one not receiving. The old output would have sent an operator to
swap a working antenna. `swing` is now a column and the tool names the channel
that ignores the emitter.

### The stall watchdog is now tunable without editing four rovers

`mavlink_radio_collection.py` has accepted `--stall-detect-seconds`,
`--stall-manual-seconds` and `--stall-progress-radius-m` all along, but
`run_capture()` in `drone_run.sh` built a fixed argument list that omitted them
— so nothing on the boot path could set one, and the 0.3 m/s implied speed floor
was not adjustable at all. `SPF_STALL_DETECT_SECONDS`,
`SPF_STALL_MANUAL_SECONDS` and `SPF_STALL_PROGRESS_RADIUS_M` now pass through
from `/etc/spf/rover_collection.env`, appear in `--print-plan`, and are refused
if set to zero — `--stall-detect-seconds 0` would stall every waypoint instantly
and the rover would spend the session escaping jams that are not there. Unset
means "use the collector's own default", so the numbers keep exactly one home.

### S-6 closed: the clock is synced before the compass gate

`reboot_rover_for_absent_compass` never returns, and the GPS clock sync ran
after it — so a rover cycling on an absent compass rebooted without ever setting
its clock, and every journal line from every iteration carried the stale Pi
clock. That journal is exactly what the loop has to be diagnosed from. The sync
now runs first; nothing in it depends on parameter sync (`--get-time` needs only
a heartbeat and a fix). Ordinary boots take the same total time either way. A
no-sky boot now spends its GPS timeout before the gate rather than after, which
is the price of a correctly stamped log.

### S-7 closed: the armed check is back, as a warning

**Correction to S-7 as written in Part 9.** That row said the removal left the
boot path with no `armed == false` assertion. That is wrong.
`prepare_vehicle_parameters()` has refused writes to an armed vehicle since
`5ec2c0e` (2026-08-01) and survived the `SPF_BOOT_VALIDATE_ONLY` removal
untouched; the refusal raises and `sync_vehicle_configuration` turns it into a
`die`. **An armed rover whose parameters differ already stops the boot today.**

What was genuinely unreported is narrower: an armed rover whose parameters
already **match**. No write happens, so nothing is unsafe — and nothing said
anything, so the rover drove off with its arm switch already on and left no
record of it. `vehicle_arm_state_gate` now runs before the parameter write and
covers that case, and gives the armed-and-differing case a diagnosis by name
instead of a generic verification failure.

It **warns** rather than dying. The dangerous case is already fatal one layer
down, so dying here would only add a new way to strand a rover for the harmless
case, on an operator error the boot path has tolerated silently for its whole
life. That trade should be made on evidence rather than by default.
The journal is correctly stamped now (S-6), so how often this actually fires is
about to become knowable — `SPF_REQUIRE_DISARMED_FOR_PARAM_SYNC=1` makes it
fatal once it is. A status read that *fails* is not treated as armed: inferring
"armed" from a missing heartbeat would strand rovers for exactly the reason the
warn-by-default choice exists.

### S-4 closed: the misdated captures now have a sidecar index

`spf/scripts/capture_time_index.py` recovers each capture's true UTC start from
its own `gps_timestamp` and writes a JSON index to a **new** location. It never
renames, opens every store `mode="r"`, and refuses to write its output inside a
directory it scanned — raw data is immutable, and correcting anything is a
separate deliberate act.

The whole reason this is not a two-line script is the timezone. Capture names
come from `datetime.fromtimestamp(...)` with no tzinfo — the rover's **local**
clock — and the fleet runs `Europe/London`, which is UTC+1 in August. Comparing
a name against the store's UTC `gps_timestamp` directly reports a 3600 s skew on
every healthy capture in the campaign. Validated against real data: correctly
named captures come out at −31 s to −89 s, which is just the lag between the
process start that names the file and the first GPS sample that lands in it.

Measured over `aug1`, `aug2`, `aug4`, `aug4_excluded`, `aug5`, `aug6`
(55 captures):

| Verdict | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| OK | 38 | within 300 s of their GPS time |
| MISDATED | 14 | worst −8 h 39 m (`RO4`, aug4_excluded); −4 h 28 m on RO1 aug5 |
| NO_GPS_TIME | 3 | the aug1 `PRODSTAB_*` bench captures — this is S-5 |

**This does not match the "19 misdated" figure quoted earlier in this report.**
That number was an estimate; this one is measured from the stores themselves,
over the directories listed above. If the earlier count covered other
directories (July captures, or the on-rover copies under `/mnt/md2/rovers`),
re-run the tool against those before treating either number as the total.

The 3 `NO_GPS_TIME` results are a useful independent confirmation of S-5: those
captures were taken with `--fake-drone`, so they have no in-content UTC and
genuinely cannot date themselves. The tool reports that rather than inventing a
time.

### S-8 closed: Bluetooth is now visible, and disable-able on demand

Measured on all four rovers: `hci0` present, `bluetooth.service` enabled. The
audit already reported the systemd *unit* (`fleet.stock.bluetooth`); it now also
reports the *radio* (`fleet.hci0`), which is the state that matters — the two
disagree whenever the unit is disabled but the boot overlay was never applied.
Read from `/sys/class/bluetooth/hci0`, not `hciconfig`: that tool is deprecated
and absent from newer images, where its absence would read as "no bluetooth
radio" — the reassuring answer, which is the one a check must never give by
accident.

`configure_rover_network.sh --stage disable-bt` appends `dtoverlay=disable-bt`,
with the same refuse-unless-the-static-address-answers guard as `disable-wifi`.
It is **not** folded into `disable-wifi` and nothing applies it automatically:
the overlay also hands `ttyAMA0` back from the Bluetooth modem, so a rover whose
flight controller is on a serial link would have its device path change
underneath it. This fleet reaches its FCs over USB, which is the only reason the
stage is offered at all — confirm per rover with `ls -l /dev/serial/by-id/`
before running it.

2.4 GHz is well out of band at 5.8 GHz, so this stays Low priority. The change
is that "nobody had looked" is no longer the same as "it is fine".

### S-9 unblocked: per-rover parameter overrides now exist

S-9 was blocked on "there is no `rover4_*.params`". The deeper reason is that
despite their names, `rover3_base_parameters.params` and
`rover3_rc_servo_parameters.params` are the **fleet** files — every rover gets
both, with `__ROVER_ID__` substituted — so nothing could be stated about one
rover only.

`sync_vehicle_configuration` now appends an optional
`rover<N>_overrides.params`, last, if it exists. Concatenation is sufficient
because `spf/mavlink/mavparm.py`'s `MAVParmDict.load()` builds a dict, making a
repeated key **last-wins**, and `diff()` compares against that merged view.
Worth stating explicitly: with *pymavlink's* own `MAVParmDict` this is not true,
and a first-wins merge would leave verification permanently unable to converge —
`sync_vehicle_configuration` would `die` on every boot of every rover. Both
directions are pinned by a test.

**No override file is shipped.** Which compass slots rover 4 should actually
declare is a statement about that vehicle's hardware, not something to infer
from a boot script. The mechanism is ready when that decision is made.
