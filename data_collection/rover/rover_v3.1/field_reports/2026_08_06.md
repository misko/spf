# Rover engineering report — 2026-08-06

Covers 2026-08-06, bench and pre-field. All times are the fleet's local time
(Europe/London) unless stated. No missions were flown; this is a preparation and
diagnosis day.

## Executive summary

**No capture data. Thirteen commits, three retracted conclusions, and one defect
that has been silently mis-dating 40% of the campaign since day one.**

Three things came out of today that change how earlier data should be read:

1. **19 of 47 finalised campaign captures carry the wrong timestamp** — every
   capture day, worst case +4h28m. Cause found and fixed (Part 3). The merged
   datasets are *not* corrupted; `v7_tx_rx_merge.py` keys on `gps_timestamp`.
   What is wrong is filenames, day attribution, and log correlation.
2. **The "channel 0 is dead fleet-wide" finding is withdrawn** (Part 2). It was
   an artifact of taking medians over a bursty emitter's silent frames. Per
   frame, every element responds to the transmitter.
3. **Rover 4's compass fault is physical and was introduced yesterday**, not a
   boot-timing race (Part 4). Rover 1's flight controller was reseated and now
   enumerates.

The fleet ends the day on `ccc1c02`, retuned to **5.840 GHz**, with all four
rovers passing or warning-only on a live-emitter signal check (Part 6).

## Part 2 — The receive-path investigation, and what it actually showed

### 2.1 WITHDRAWN: "channel 0 is deaf on every radio, fleet-wide"

Earlier today I reported that ch0 had never received the emitter on any rover on
any day, supported by an emitter-band SNR of 0–2 dB across 31 receiver-captures,
and a coherence between elements at the emitter IF of ~0.005.

**That is wrong, and the method was wrong.** The O4 is a bursty digital video
transmitter, not a CW source — `data_quality_plan.md` records 60–70% NaN as the
healthy baseline for it. Every statistic I computed took a **median over all
frames**, so with the emitter silent in most frames the median measured the gaps
between bursts, not the signal. The "noise reference" band I chose also sat
inside the emitter's own 3 MHz bandwidth.

Per frame, the picture inverts:

| receiver | ch0 burst swing | ch0 loud−quiet | ch1 swing | corr(ch0,ch1) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| RO1 r0 | 19.8 dB | +12.8 | 13.9 | +0.71 |
| RO1 r1 | 20.5 dB | +11.5 | 10.6 | +0.61 |
| RO3 r0 | 19.6 dB | +11.4 | 14.2 | +0.64 |
| RO3 r1 | 20.4 dB | +12.0 | 10.9 | +0.68 |
| RO4 r0 | 18.3 dB | +10.7 | 12.2 | +0.36 |
| **RO4 r1** | **3.3 dB** | **+2.0** | 9.8 | +0.54 |

ch0 swings ~20 dB between burst and gap and is the *more* responsive element on
every healthy receiver; both channels rise and fall together. The one genuinely
flat element was **RO4 r1 ch0** — 3.3 dB swing, the Aug 4 missing-pole antenna.

### 2.2 WITHDRAWN: "ch0 died when the fleet moved to 5.766 GHz"

Follow-on claim, same method, same error. Checked against the 5.866 GHz and
2.467 GHz eras it appeared to hold, but per-frame those eras show ch0 bursting
at 16.7–18.9 dB — identical to 5.766. The earlier campaign also used a
**CW "tone blaster"** emitter, present in *every* frame, which is why medians
showed signal there and not on the O4 days. **There is no frequency effect.**

### 2.3 What does stand

- `rover radio signal` compares channels **across all frames**, so with a bursty
  emitter it measures the silence. This is the root of every wrong turn above
  and it is still unfixed — see S-6.
- The metric it calls `railed_fraction` is `(gain == max(gain)).mean()`, i.e.
  the fraction of frames at the *maximum observed* gain, **not** at the hardware
  ceiling. For a constant gain it reports 100% regardless. It is a good
  frozen-AGC detector wearing the wrong name (Part 6.2).

## Part 3 — Capture filenames have been wrong since day one of the campaign

### 3.1 The measurement

Comparing every finalised campaign capture's filename against the minimum
`gps_timestamp` inside its own store:

| day | misdated >3 min | worst |
|---|---:|---|
| 07-31 | 3 | +2h03m |
| 08-01 | 3 | +57m |
| 08-02 | 4 | +51m |
| 08-04 | 5 | **+8h38m** |
| 08-05 | 4 | +4h27m |
| **total** | **19 of 47** | |

Rover 4's 08-04 entries are mostly the separate PDT-vs-BST timezone bug (O3,
since fixed by the reconciler); the rest are the defect below.

### 3.2 The cause

The Pi 4 has no battery-backed RTC, so a network-less boot restores the
timestamp saved at last shutdown (journald: `System clock time unset or jumped
backwards, restoring from recorded timestamp`). The capture filename is stamped
from that clock at process start (`mavlink_radio_collection.py:254`) — minutes
before the first sample is recorded.

`drone_run.sh` then declined to ask the GPS:

```bash
system_clock_is_plausible() {
    # Raspberry Pi 5 has an RTC and may also have NTP. A plausible clock is
    # sufficient for boot filenames; ...
    [[ "$(date +%s)" -ge 1735689600 ]]  # 2025-01-01T00:00:00Z
}
```

Both premises are false for this fleet: these are **Pi 4s** (no RTC), and
"plausible" meant "later than 1 Jan 2025", which a clock restored to four hours
ago clears by nineteen months. Introduced by `920bc63` *"Speed rover boot to
navigation wait"* (2026-07-31 05:54) as a boot-latency optimisation — the first
day of the v7 campaign.

The rover was carrying correct UTC on the MAVLink link the whole time
(`SYSTEM_TIME.time_unix_usec`, from ArduPilot's GPS) and writing it into every
snapshot, while naming the file from a clock read off disk.

### 3.3 The blast radius, precisely

**Merged datasets are sound.** `v7_tx_rx_merge.py` interpolates TX position onto
RX `gps_timestamp`, never onto filenames. Bearings and ground truth are correct.

**What is broken** is filenames as an ordering or identification key, day
attribution (Part 11 of the Aug 5 report already counts by GPS date for this
reason), and correlating a capture to journal entries. The merge is also a full
TX×RX cross-product over whatever it is handed — it does no session curation —
so *selecting* captures by filename can silently mis-group them.

### 3.4 Fixed

Every boot now obtains GPS time before anything is named, and again before each
capture. Deliberately unbounded in attempts is *not* the design: three bounded
tries, then a loud warning and a boot that continues, because a rover with no
GPS cannot start a mission anyway.

A latent epoch-0 bug had to be closed at the same time, because making the sync
mandatory promoted it from unreachable to per-boot-reachable: `--get-time` used
to exit on a 3D fix even with no UTC, writing epoch 0, and the shell's `1970-*`
guard is timezone-dependent (`1970-01-01 01:00:00` in Europe/London but
`1969-12-31 16:00:00` west of UTC, where it misses entirely). Both layers fixed;
validation is now on a parsed epoch.

## Part 4 — Rover 4's external compass

Symptom: MAVLink comes up, then `PreArm: Compass not healthy`; on one boot the
external IST8310 (device ID 658953) was absent entirely and the policy gate
failed, killing the collector.

### 4.1 It is physical, and it was introduced on 2026-08-06

| | Aug 5 (6 boots) | Aug 6 (last 4 boots) |
|---|---|---|
| Compass policy PASS | **6 / 6** | gate ran once → **FAILED** |
| `PreArm: Compass not healthy` | **0** | **3 of 4 boots** |
| External 658953 absent | 0 | 1 boot |

Same firmware, same parameters, same calibration (offset norm 1009.7 mG on all
seven recorded occurrences — no recalibration). The only change in between was
physical work on the vehicle. A boot-timing race would have failed yesterday too.

### 4.2 Rover 4 has exactly one compass

`COMPASS_PRIO2_ID=658945` (I2C bus 0) and `PRIO3_ID=131594` (SPI) are configured
but have **never** been detected; `COMPASS_USE2/USE3` are both 0, so they are
inert warnings. Decoded, 658953 = `0xA0E09` = IST8310 on I2C bus 1, addr 0x0E,
external. When it drops there is no fallback — hence "not healthy" rather than a
silent demotion to an internal compass. The phantom IDs match rover 3's own
parameter dumps, suggesting rover 4's FC inherited them at bring-up.

### 4.3 The gate now retries, and only an absence is retried

`compass_policy` classifies its own errors. `retryable` is true only when
**every** error is "the external compass is not on the bus". A wrong
`COMPASS_PRIO1_ID`, a nonzero `COMPASS_DISBLMSK` or an out-of-limit offset reads
identically after any number of reboots, so retrying it would bury a real defect
behind a delay. An absence *plus* a misconfiguration is not retryable.

Ladder: one check, then 3 retries — settle 5–10 s and re-read parameters (no
reboot), then FC reboot + 15–25 s, then FC reboot + 30–45 s. Delays are
randomised so retries cannot land in lockstep with a periodic condition, and so
four rovers restarting together do not retry in phase. The first attempt does
not reboot because the gate reads `COMPASS_DEV_ID*`, which the FC populates
during its boot probe — a check run shortly after an FC restart can report a
healthy compass as absent, and ruling that out costs seconds rather than ~40 s.

Then it escalates to a **full rover reboot**, unbounded by request, with a 20 s
announced window so an operator can intervene. `SPF_COMPASS_REBOOT_ENABLE=0`
parks a rover with a known-bad lead instead of cycling.

## Part 6 — Live-emitter signal checks, 5.840 GHz

First fleet-wide check with the emitter on at the new carrier. All four rovers,
100 frames, motion-free.

| | ch0 @ant | ch1 @ant | gains | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RO1** r0 | −50.9 | −43.9 | 36.8 / 29.9 | ch0 7.0 dB low → WARN |
| RO1 r1 | −46.7 | −45.1 | 32.7 / 31.4 | 1.6 dB → OK |
| **RO2** r0 | −24.5 | −23.0 | 10.8 / 8.9 | 1.5 dB → **PASS** |
| **RO3** r0 | −45.7 | −49.4 | 28.1 / 31.1 | 3.7 dB → OK |
| RO3 r1 | −46.8 | −47.5 | 29.2 / 29.1 | 0.7 dB → **PASS** |
| **RO4** r0 | −43.1 | −50.7 | 29.1 / 36.5 | ch1 7.7 dB low → WARN |
| RO4 r1 | −43.4 | −47.9 | 29.4 / 34.5 | 4.5 dB → OK |

### 6.1 The gain column is the headline

RO1 r0 ch0 in its Aug 5 *mission* capture read **−83.2 dB at 61.4 dB gain,
railed 90%**. It now reads **−50.9 at 36.8 dB, railed 1%**, and its imbalance is
7.0 dB rather than 26.7 dB.

Caution against over-reading that: on Aug 5 the weak channel sat at the noise
floor with the AGC railed, and you cannot measure how far down something is once
it is below the floor. A strong local emitter lifts it off, and the *true*
imbalance shows. Some of 27 dB → 7 dB is the signal being stronger, not the path
being better. The residual 6–9 dB matches the fleet baseline already documented
in `rx_signal_metrics.py` ("6-11 dB as of 2026-08-04").

Note also that the weak channel **flips side** between rovers — ch0 on RO1, ch1
on RO3 and RO4 — which rules out a systematic per-channel-index deficit.

### 6.2 Rover 3: a frozen AGC, and a wrong diagnosis

RO3 r1 ch1 (**B1**, stern) initially reported `railed 100%` at a median gain of
29 dB. The raw gains across 100 frames:

```
r0 ch0 (A0):  {28:86, 29:13, 30:1}                           AGC working
r0 ch1 (A1):  {35:88, 36:1, 37:10, 40:1}                     AGC working
r1 ch0 (B0):  {26:20, 27:35, 28:23, 29:14, 30:3, 33:3, 34:1, 62:1}
r1 ch1 (B1):  {29:100}                                       FROZEN
```

The gain was **constant at 29 dB for every frame** — not at the 62 dB rail.

I attributed this to the fast_attack gain-lock the config comment warns about
(*"persistent fast-attack gain-lock states seen on bursty digital video"*).
**That was wrong.** An antenna was replaced, `fast_attack` was left unchanged,
and the channel now varies normally (railed 5%), r1 imbalance 4.3 → 0.7 dB and
r0 9.0 → 3.7 dB. The rover PASSES.

The mechanism makes sense in hindsight: an antenna not coupling to the field
gives the AGC nothing varying to track, so the loop settles and stays. The A/B
is therefore uncontaminated and rover 3 remains the fast_attack arm.

## Part 7 — The fleet was not on the code it appeared to be on

Rovers 1 and 2 spent the evening on `ff268d5`, still tuned to **5.766 GHz**,
while the emitter had moved to 5.840. Their journals said:

```
Git remote unavailable after 12s;
```

Measured on rover 1:

```
22:54:47.510  Reached target network.target
22:54:47.539  Starting spf-rover-update.service          <- 29 ms later
22:54:48.170  dhcp4 (eth1): new lease 192.168.2.10       <- a PLUTO
22:54:48.310  Reached target network-online.target       <- satisfied by it
22:54:50.965  device (eth0): carrier: link connected     <- the LAN, +3.4 s
```

The 12 s Git budget counted against an interface that did not exist yet. And
`network-online.target` is no defence: the Plutos' USB-net interfaces come up in
under a second **with a global IPv4**, so the target goes green while the LAN is
dark. Verified — eth1 reports `carrier=1` and a global address exactly as eth0
does.

Fixed by gating on the named LAN interface (`/sys/class/net/eth0/carrier` plus a
global-scope IPv4 on that device) before the fetch clock starts, bounded both
ways so an offline field boot gives up after an 8 s carrier grace rather than
burning the full budget. Still fails open.

**Auto-reboot-after-pull was never broken** — `request_reboot` fires whenever a
fast-forward lands, and rover 4 demonstrated it (22:31:31 pull → self-reboot →
22:32:33 on new code). The failure was always the fetch, not the reboot.
